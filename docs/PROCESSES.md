# Processes: what the app does with reminders

The model ([CSDP.md](CSDP.md), [`Model/Logbook.orm`](../Model/Logbook.orm);
its listing, `Logbook.forml`, is generated by the build) says what may be
recorded of schedules and reminders, and what must hold of it. It does
not say what happens when: that a reminder is closed when an event does
its work, that the schedule's next one is then made, that one closed by a
record entered in error opens again. Those are processes,
carried out by the app's service layer, one change at a time; the model's
rules are checked on what each change leaves (the generated code, at
save). None of them is a derivation, and none is in the model.

**What carries them out.** `Shared/LBProcedures.h`: categories on the
model's generated classes, Foundation and Core Data only, each changing
objects in a context and nothing else (no save, no request); the
generated code checks what one leaves when it is saved, as for any change.
The same code runs wherever the logbook is kept:

- **The server** runs each as an OData action, bound to the entity it acts
  on (`Server/LBActions.h`), carried out and saved whole in the request,
  or refused with the rule it breaks and nothing of it saved:

  | Action | Bound to | Parameters | Answers | Who may |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | `Start` | a schedule | `LastDoneAt`, `LastDoneMileage`, `LastDoneMileageUnit` | its first reminder | the owner |
  | `Complete` | a reminder | `Event` | the next reminder, or none | whoever drives the vehicle |
  | `Reschedule` | a reminder | `DueDate`, `DueMileage`, `DueMileageUnit`, `Note` | the rescheduling | whoever drives the vehicle |
  | `Cancel` | a reminder | `CancelDate`, `ReplacementDueDate`, `ReplacementDueMileage`, `ReplacementDueMileageUnit` | the replacement, or none | the owner |
  | `Discard` | a record | `KeepNextReminder` | nothing | the owner |

  (`POST Records(<id>)/Default.Complete`, and so on; a unit is its code,
  an event a reference; every parameter but `Complete`'s `Event` may be
  left out.) A refusal is a
  `400`; `Discard` of an event whose next reminder was touched since is a
  `409`, its code `NextReminderTouched`, its target that reminder's id
  (9). And whenever the server saves events, whichever device sent them
  and however (a POST, a change set, a sync), it first closes the
  reminders they do the work of (automatic completion, 4), in the same
  save, before the model's rules are checked.
- **The desktop app** is online: its Reminders tab calls these actions
  (ODataKit's `ODataOperationCall`) and reads back what they changed; an
  event it saves in the Records tab closes reminders on the server as it
  is saved.
- **The iOS app** is local-first: it keeps its own store, runs the same
  procedures in it as the user acts, offline or not, and before each save
  the same automatic completion and rules as the server; it syncs the
  objects they made and changed, as it syncs any record (Offline, below).
  It queues no actions or intents: what a procedure does is in its
  objects, and since they are named by what they are made of (below),
  another device that did the same makes the same objects, which the sync
  meets as one.

**Deterministic.** Whatever a procedure makes is named by what it is made
of: its id is a name-based UUID (version 5: SHA-1 of Logbook's namespace,
`3dfab34a-71f4-530a-98a1-5e6711f543a6`, and a name), the name saying the
source object's id (in lower case) and the purpose:

| What is made | Its name |
|---|---|
| a schedule's first reminder | `start of <schedule id>` (`... after <n>`, when the schedule had n reminders before) |
| a reminder's next (5) | `next of <reminder id>` |
| a cancelled reminder's replacement (8) | `replacement of <reminder id>` |
| a reminder's nth rescheduling (6) | `<reminder id> rescheduled <n>` |

So two devices that close the same reminder make the same next one, and
whoever reads the id can tell what made it (Python's
`uuid.uuid5(namespace, name)` gives the same). Where a procedure chooses
among several (the latest event of the work, the reminder an event
closes), it chooses by moment, then by id, never by the order objects
were read in. A day of the model (due on, cancelled on) is a UTC day, as
its queries' `date()` and `addMonths()` have it.

The facts below are the model's, as the verbalizer says them.

## 1. Set up a schedule

The owner plans recurring work for a vehicle: a task (an oil change), a
kind of expense (the parking permit) or a checklist (the monthly
inspection), every so far, every so many months, or both, whichever comes
first.

The service layer records the schedule:
- Schedule is of Vehicle; Schedule was entered by User;
- Schedule is for Task, or for ExpenseKind, or for Checklist (exactly
  one);
- Schedule recurs after Distance, and/or recurs after Duration (at least
  one);
- maybe Schedule has Note.

Then it makes the schedule's first reminder (2).

A schedule is changed by going on in another from its open reminder on
(11): its past stays as it was.

## 2. Make a schedule's first reminder

Due from now, or from the last time the work was done, as the owner says
(the oil was last changed at 148,000 km on 2026-06-01).

The service layer works out where it is due from: the moment and distance
given, else the latest event of the vehicle that did the work (a service
with an item of the task, an expense of the kind, an inspection through
the checklist), else now and the vehicle's latest distance. It adds the
schedule's intervals: the distance interval to the distance, the months
to the date (as `ReminderNextDue` does for the date). It records:
- Reminder comes from Schedule; Reminder is of Vehicle; Reminder was
  entered by User;
- Reminder is due at Distance, where the schedule recurs after a distance;
- Reminder is due on Date, where it recurs after a duration.

It is set now ("Reminder was set at Moment"): an event closes a reminder
only from the day it was set (4). Where a distance is to be counted from
and none is known (no distance given, none recorded of the vehicle), the
reminder is due on its date alone; a schedule that recurs after a distance
only is then refused, until the owner says one. A schedule with an open
reminder is not started again.

A reminder from a schedule is of the schedule's vehicle: the service layer
records it so, here and wherever it makes one (5), an entered fact like any
record's vehicle. The model does not derive it; it checks it ("If some
Reminder comes from some Schedule and that Reminder is of some Vehicle then
that Schedule is of that Vehicle").

It records where the reminder is counted from: "Reminder is counted from
Date" (the day it was worked out from), and "Reminder is counted from
Distance" where the schedule recurs after a distance and one was known.
Every reminder of a schedule is counted from a day; a one-off is counted
from nothing. A change of the schedule counts it again from there (11).

The schedule then has one open reminder (`OneOpenReminder`).

## 3. A one-off reminder

The owner asks to be reminded once: have the air conditioning checked by
2027-05-01. The service layer records a reminder with no schedule:
- Reminder is of Vehicle; Reminder was entered by User;
- Reminder is for Task, or for ExpenseKind, or for Checklist (exactly
  one, as a schedule's work would be);
- Reminder is due at Distance and/or is due on Date;
- maybe Reminder has Note.

A vehicle may have any number of open reminders: one per schedule, and
one-offs.

## 4. An event does a reminder's work: it is closed

A service is saved with an item of an oil change; an expense of the
permit's kind; an inspection through the checklist. Saving it (adding the
event, or changing it so that it now does the work), the service layer
finds the open reminders of the event's vehicle that the event does the
work of: for a schedule's reminder, the schedule's work; for a one-off,
its own. For each, in the same change:
- Reminder was done by Event;
- Reminder was done at Moment: the event's moment;
- Reminder was done at Distance: the event's distance, where it has one
  (an expense need not).

The rules hold it to that: a reminder done by an event was done at the
event's moment (`ReminderDoneAtItsEventsMoment`) and at its distance where
it has one (`ReminderDoneAtItsEventsMileage`); done by an event of its
vehicle; done at a distance only with a moment.

A reminder is done only by an event: there is no closing by hand. The
owner may say which event closed one (Mark Done, `Complete`): one that may
close it (below); what then closes it is what settling says, the earliest
that may.

For a schedule's reminder, the service layer then makes the next (5).

**Settling, exactly: automatic completion.** Before the server saves, the
vehicles of the events in the save that are new, or changed in their
moment, vehicle, distance, kind, checklist or items (a service's item
added, or changed to another task) are settled, and a vehicle an event
moved from too: their reminders are made what entering all their events
afresh would make them, in order (by moment, then id). So the outcome
never depends on the order events were entered, or changed, or synced:
an event moved earlier or later, to another vehicle, or no longer of the
work, leaves what entering the events as they now are gives.

- An event may close a reminder that is of its vehicle, for work it does
  (its schedule's, or its own), not cancelled, and set on or before the
  event's day (the UTC days of the two moments).
- A schedule's run: from its first reminder, each event of its work, in
  order, closes the reminder open then, if it may, and the next is made
  due from it (5); the next event then meets that one. Past a cancelled
  reminder the run goes on to its replacement; past one done with no event
  (as a plain `DELETE` of its event leaves it) to its next, both as they
  are; where a run ends (no next made), at the schedule's next first
  reminder, when it was started again.
- A one-off reminder: the first event that may close it.
- A reminder the run closes otherwise than it is now is closed so, or
  reopened; a next reminder the run no longer reaches is deleted; one
  whose reminder before is now closed by another event, or at another
  moment or distance, is due anew from it.
- A next reminder the owner has touched (moved, noted, given an
  attachment, cancelled, replacing or replaced) is not changed or deleted
  so: the save is refused (`409`, `NextReminderTouched`), nothing changed,
  for the owner to undo the move or the touch. Discarding a record is the
  one place they may say to go ahead (9).

So two events of the same work close one reminder each, the earlier the
earlier, whichever was saved first: the second closes the next reminder
the first made. Day-granular as it is, an event the same day a schedule
was started may close its first reminder at once, even the event it was
counted from; the next is then due as the first was.

## 5. The schedule's next reminder

When a schedule's reminder is done, the service layer makes the next, in
the same change: due its intervals after when and where the last was done.
- Reminder comes from Schedule (the same); Reminder is of Vehicle (the
  schedule's, as in 2); Reminder was entered by User;
- Reminder is due on Date: the date of the done moment plus the
  schedule's months (`ReminderNextDue`);
- Reminder is due at Distance: the event's distance plus the schedule's
  interval, in the event's unit;
- Reminder is counted from Date: the day of the done moment; Reminder is
  counted from Distance: the event's distance.

A cancelled reminder's replacement (8) is due where the owner says, and
counted from where the one it replaces was.

For a schedule that recurs after a distance, the event's distance is
needed: closed by an event at none (an expense with no odometer reading),
the reminder is closed all the same, and no next is made; the schedule is
left with none open, to start again. The desktop app warns before saving
an event at no distance that would close such a reminder (the owner may
save it anyway).

## 6. Postpone or move a reminder

The owner moves a reminder to another date or distance, whether it is
overdue yet or not: away in May, the air conditioning in June. The
reminder keeps what it is due at now; what it was due at before is
recorded with the move:
- Rescheduling is of Reminder; Rescheduling was made at Moment (now);
  Rescheduling was made by User;
- Rescheduling moved from Date: the due date before, where it changed;
- Rescheduling moved from Distance: the due distance before, where it
  changed (at least one of the two);
- maybe Rescheduling has Note (why);
- and the reminder's Reminder is due on Date / is due at Distance,
  changed.

Only an open reminder is moved, and only to somewhere it is not due
already. Where it gains a date (or a distance) it was not due at, what it
was due at, the other, is what the move records it moved from.

## 7. Overdue and late

Queries, not processes: the model has them, and the app runs them.
- **Overdue:** open (neither done nor cancelled), and due on a day before
  today (`OverdueReminders`), or at a distance the vehicle has reached, in
  kilometres whatever the units (`OverdueByMileage`).
- **Late:** done or cancelled after the day it was due (`LateReminders`,
  with the day it was closed), or done past the distance it was due at
  (`DoneLateByMileage`).

## 8. Cancel a reminder

The owner skips one (no inspection this month) or gives up on it (the car
is sold before the permit runs out). The service layer records:
- Reminder was cancelled on Date (today, or the day given).

A cancelled reminder is not done too (an exclusion), and its schedule has
no open reminder until the next is made: the owner may make one at once,
due when they say, which replaces the one cancelled:
- the new reminder, as in 2 or 3 (of the same vehicle: a reminder
  replaces only one of its own vehicle);
- Reminder replaces Reminder: the new replaces the cancelled.

A reminder replaces at most one and is replaced by at most one, and no
chain of them comes back to itself. Only an open reminder is cancelled;
its replacement is set now, for its work: from its schedule, where it has
one.

## 9. A record entered in error is discarded

The owner deletes a service entered by mistake. In the same change, the
service layer settles the record's vehicle without it (4), then deletes
it: what it closed is closed by the next event that may, or reopened, and
a next reminder its closing made is deleted, or made due anew from the
event that now closes the one before it.

Where such a next one has been touched (the owner moved it, noted
something on it, cancelled it), the procedure stops before changing
anything, and the app asks the owner what to do: nothing is deleted or
reopened until they say. Deleting it, or making it due anew, would lose
what they did to it. So the owner chooses (`Discard` again, with
`KeepNextReminder` false): delete it too (or make it due anew), with its
moves and attachments; or keep the record. Keeping the next reminder and
discarding the record (`KeepNextReminder` true) is refused: a reminder is
done only by an event, and the one the record closed would be left done
by nothing, or open beside the kept one.

The desktop app removes an event that closed reminders so (Remove, in the
Records tab), and asks the owner on the `409`; any other record it
deletes as before. A plain `DELETE` of an event leaves what it closed done,
with no event, as it is.

An event changed so that it no longer does the work (a service whose oil
change item is removed) is settled the same way (4), the event kept.

## 10. Two devices, offline, both make the next reminder

Anna and Ben both log the oil change offline, on two phones; each phone
closes the reminder and makes the schedule's next (4, 5). Both make the
same next reminder: its id is named by the reminder it follows ("next of
…"), so what the second phone sends is the same object, not a second open
one beside it. The server, saving each event as it arrives, settles the
vehicle (4): the reminder closed by the earlier of the two, the next by
the later, whichever came first; two records of the same work are then
the owner's to keep or discard (9).

Where the two phones made the same object with the same values, the sync
meets it as one, and says nothing. Where they made it, or changed it,
differently, it is a conflict (below): the server's stands, and the phone
keeps its own for the user to settle.

## 11. Change a schedule

A schedule is a rule its reminders are made by, one at a time. Its done
and cancelled reminders are what happened, made by the rule as it was;
its open one, and those to come, are what is planned. The owner changes
the rule: its work, its intervals, its note (the desktop's Edit
Schedule). What happened stays as it is; what is planned follows.

So a change is a new schedule going on from the old one, from its open
reminder on:
- Schedule is a continuation of Schedule (the old one); the new one is of
  the same vehicle (checked: "If some Schedule1 is a continuation of some
  Schedule2 that is of some Vehicle then Schedule1 is of that Vehicle"),
  entered by the owner, with the work, intervals and note as changed;
- the old schedule keeps its done and cancelled reminders, with its work
  and intervals as they were: which events did them is worked out from
  the old schedule's work still (4), so changing the work undoes nothing;
- the old schedule has no reminder open after (`ContinuedHaveNoOpenReminder`),
  and makes no next: its events past the change close what the new one
  has open.

The open reminder goes on with it, as it is or anew:
- **Not touched** (neither moved, noted, given an attachment, nor a
  replacement): it comes from the new schedule now, counted from where it
  was (its Reminder is counted from Date and Distance), due anew by the
  new intervals: the day plus the new months, the distance plus the new
  interval. Its id stays (it is the same reminder, planned otherwise).
- **Touched** (moved by hand, say): what the owner decided was for the
  old rule. It is cancelled (Reminder was cancelled on Date: today), and
  the new schedule is started from where it was counted from, its first
  reminder due by the new intervals (2). The app says so before the
  change is saved: the move is not carried over.
- **None open** (a schedule that recurs after a distance, closed by an
  event at none (5)): the new schedule is started as in 2.

Its ids are named as the procedures name what they make: the new schedule
"continuation of" the old one's id, its first reminder as a start of it.
The server does it as the action `Continue`, bound to the schedule
changed (`Server/LBActions.h`), its owner's to call; a schedule changed
already is refused (the one it goes on in is changed instead). The
desktop lists the schedules in force, those nothing goes on from.

Automatic completion (4) works each schedule's run as before, with two
differences: a schedule gone on in another makes no next past its own
reminders (its run ends there), and the events that did the reminders of
the schedules a schedule goes on from are theirs, not its own (the event a
reminder that went on was counted from does not close it again).
A schedule changed again goes on in a third; the chain is one series to
whoever looks at it (the calendar, 12).

## 12. What is planned, and when

What a schedule has planned past its open reminder is not recorded: it
is worked out when it is shown, from the rule, so a changed schedule
plans otherwise at once.
- **By date:** the open reminder's due date, plus the months, again and
  again.
- **By distance:** the open reminder's due distance, plus the interval;
  on a calendar, on the day the vehicle is expected to reach it, by the
  distance it has gone a day over its last 90 days of odometer readings
  (an estimate, said as one). With too few readings to tell, it is listed
  by its distance, on no day.
- **Both:** whichever comes first.
- So far ahead: a year from today.

The desktop's calendar (to come) shows a vehicle's reminders on their
days: done (by which event, its driver; late or not), cancelled (and what
replaced it), open (due, overdue, moved from where), and planned (as
estimated where they are); each record on its day besides. Working out
what is planned is procedure, shared by both apps; nothing of it is
saved.

## Offline: the phone

What syncs, and which way, is the model's: its Core Data mapping in
`Model/Logbook.orm` says it (`ormtool sync`), and ORMKit writes it into the
generated model, which the server and the phone both load:

| Which way | What |
|---|---|
| down: the server's, read | units, kinds of cost, exchange rates |
| both: changed on either side | users, vehicles and their sharing and valuations, records (every kind, schedules and reminders among them) and their items, answers and attachments, reschedulings, checklists and their questions and options |

Each object a phone changes carries ODataSync's bookkeeping, added by the
mapping and not part of the conceptual model: its version vector
(`syncVersions`: what each device's changes it has seen, so an older copy
is never taken for a newer one and only changes made without knowing of
each other conflict), and the stamp of its last change (`syncModified`).
The server keeps both, as it keeps any property, and its ODataSyncService
compares what a device sends with what it has.

**When it syncs.** As the app opens and comes back, two seconds after a
save (once for saves close together), every minute, and when sync is
switched back on. Each sync brings down what changed at the server that
the user may see, then sends what waits, oldest first. With sync off, or
no network, what is saved waits; the Vehicle tab says how many changes
wait, and how many the server refused (with its words: sent again, or
forgotten).

A change the server refuses because what came with it had not arrived yet
(a reminder's next, sent before the reminder it follows was closed there)
is sent again once, at the end of the same sync, after the rest.

**Conflicts.** A change made on the phone to an object the server changed
too, since they last agreed, is a conflict. The sync never waits on one:
the server's version stands, there and on the phone, and what the phone
changed otherwise than the server did is kept as a pending conflict, on
the phone alone (an app-state store beside the logbook's, never synced,
no part of the model): which object, which properties, mine and theirs,
when. Bookkeeping, a value the phone left unsaid, and the same value on
both sides (the same procedure, carried out on both) are no conflict. The
app shows how many wait (a badge on the Vehicle tab, a button over the
lists), and for each, the user chooses:

- **Keep theirs:** what the phone had is given up; the conflict is
  forgotten.
- **Keep mine:** the phone's values are written back over the server's,
  as a new change: saved here (the rules checked), and synced, so the
  server and every other device take them. An object the server deleted
  is made again.
- **Edit by hand:** the record's form, as it now is (the server's); what
  the user saves settles it, and syncs.

A record deleted at the server and changed on the phone stays deleted
until the user keeps theirs (it goes) or keeps mine (it comes back); one
deleted on the phone and changed at the server comes back, and the user
may delete it again.

**Receipts.** A photo of a receipt is an Attachment of its record, named
by its id: its content the photo, a JPEG at most 2000 pixels on its
longest side, which syncs with it both ways, as any of its properties
(ODataSync carries it as `Edm.Binary`, base64 in the JSON of the change
set; Core Data keeps it in a file beside the store, and the sync's own
bookkeeping keeps its digest, not the photo).

**Signing out** deletes the logbook on the phone (it is the user's). What
it would lose is said first: the changes not sent yet and the conflicts
not settled, how many. Sync Now sends them, sync switched off or not, and
signs out when that leaves nothing to lose (a conflict is still the
user's to settle, and is said again); Sign Out Anyway loses them; Cancel
keeps the phone signed in. With nothing to lose it signs out at once.
